Short, regular practice beats long sessions. Ten minutes most days is plenty.

The idea behind quarter

Every question comes with a hint, worked steps and feedback for that exact question, so what a child reads always fits what they're working on. Topics come back later, a little at a time, so what's learned stays learned. Children are never rushed on normal practice. The only timed activities are clearly labelled: the 11+ practice papers and the times tables challenge.

Using quarter

How quarter works

  1. A short check-up finds the starting pointAbout 24 questions that adapt to your child, so the first plan fits them, not just their school year.
  2. A weekly plan keeps things steadyEach week focuses on a few topics, with review of earlier ones mixed in.
  3. Practice is short and kindA hint is always there, “Show the steps” explains a worked answer, and an optional AI tutor can talk a problem through without giving the answer away.
  4. Working-out tools help on paper and on screenCounters, number lines, grids and method sheets are a tap away, and the keyboard works everywhere.
  5. Topics return at the right momentAnything that was tricky comes back sooner. Anything secure comes back later.
  6. You see the progressThe parents' area shows what's secure, what needs attention, and a progress report you can print or save for any dates you choose.

What the status words mean

  • New: not tried yet.
  • Learning: being worked on.
  • Getting there: mostly right, still wobbly.
  • Got it: secure, and brought back later to stay that way.

Where it works

In any modern browser on phones, tablets and computers. Progress is saved to your account, so a child can carry on from any device.

Number

Number

Factors, multiples, highest common factor and lowest common multiple.

Ask: How can you tell quickly whether a number is a multiple of 3?

Fractions of amounts

Number

Find a fraction of a number or quantity.

Ask: What does the bottom number tell you, and what does the top number tell you?

Mixed numbers and improper fractions

Number

Count the fraction pieces in whole numbers and mixed numbers.

Ask: Why does the denominator stay the same?

Multiplying fractions by whole numbers

Number

Multiply a fraction by a whole number, and write the answer as a mixed number.

Ask: Why does the bottom number not change when you multiply?

Numbers to a million

Number

Read, write and compare numbers to 1,000,000, and multiply and divide by 10, 100 and 1,000.

Ask: What happens to the digits when you multiply by 1,000?

Prime, square and cube numbers

Number

Ask: How can you tell that 21 is not a prime number?

Thousandths

Number

Read, write and compare decimals with three decimal places, and relate thousandths to tenths and hundredths.

Ask: Why is 0.4 the same as 0.400?

Converting units

Measures

Ask: Should the number get bigger or smaller when you change kilograms into grams? Why?

Beyond maths

How the STEM lab works

The lab sits next to the maths activities, with its own sticky note on Today. It has its own topics and progress, separate from the maths plan, so children can explore for the fun of it.

Computing

Precise instructions, loops, sorting and searching, binary and hexadecimal, tracing code, logic, storage and secret codes.

Data science

Reading survey data, spotting outliers and checking whether data is fair.

AI and machine learning

Learning from examples, bias in data, nearest neighbour, a tiny artificial neuron, accuracy, spam filters, gradient descent and a neural network layer.

Quantum ideas

Packets of energy, qubits and probability, how many states qubits hold, and the X and H gates.

Build it labs

Binary lamps. Switch lamps on and off to build numbers.

Program the robot. Queue up instructions to reach the star.

Teach a machine. Place dots and see how a nearest-neighbour model decides.

Qubit playground. Try the X and H gates and measure the qubit.

Practical tips

Helping at home

Before practice

  • Pick a calm moment, and stop when focus fades.
  • Two or three topics are enough for a sitting.
  • Have paper and a pencil to hand.

During practice

  • Let them try first. A hint is always there.
  • Ask them to explain a step in their own words.
  • Praise the effort and the method, not just the answer.

When something's tricky

  • Use “Show the steps”, then try a fresh question.
  • Use a real object: coins, a ruler, slices of fruit.
  • Come back to it tomorrow. Topics return on their own.

After practice

  • Look at the “Ask” question for the topic.
  • Spot the topic in real life: shopping, cooking, travel.
  • Check the parents' area now and then, and celebrate what's secure.
If a topic feels too easy or too hard, try one from the year above or below. The map is there to be explored.

Keeping in touch

Questions, ideas and feedback are always welcome through the feedback form in the parents' area, or at yourquarter.co.uk.
