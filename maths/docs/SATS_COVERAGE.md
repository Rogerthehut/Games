# How quarter lines up with the Key Stage 2 maths tests

Generated by `npx tsx scripts/sats-coverage.ts` from `src/curriculum/sats-map.ts`. Checked by `src/curriculum/sats-map.test.ts`.

**What this is.** The mark schemes for the 2024 and 2025 Key Stage 2 maths tests (published by the Standards and Testing Agency on gov.uk) say, for every question, which part of the national curriculum it assessed, as a content-domain code such as `6C7a`. This lists every code the two tests used, how many questions used it, and which of our topics practise it. It records only question numbers and codes. No test question or wording is copied here or anywhere in the repository.

**Result.** 178 question parts across 6 papers used 124 codes. **117 are fully practised and 7 partly.** Topic names are checked by the tests, but the mapping was made by hand and still needs a teacher's review.

## What the comparison found

Reading the arithmetic papers against our question generators (the shapes of the sums, not just the topic names) found four real gaps, all now fixed, and one overstated claim:

1. **Long multiplication (6C7a).** Both tests have two long multiplications in the arithmetic paper (three digits by two digits and four digits by two digits). Written multiplication stopped at two digits by two digits. *Fixed:* levels 4 and 5 of *Multiplying* are now three digits by two and four digits by two.
2. **Four-digit short division (5C7b).** The tests divide four-digit numbers by one digit (such as 6,600 ÷ 6). Our division reached three digits by one digit. *Fixed:* levels 4 and 5 of *Dividing*, the second with a remainder.
3. **A shorter second number (3C2, 4C2, 5C2).** The tests add and subtract numbers of different lengths (a four-digit number minus a two-digit one). Ours always had matching lengths. *Fixed:* about half of the questions in *Adding and subtracting* now have a shorter second number, and the estimate step rounds each number to its first digit.
4. **Taking fractions away, and mixed numbers (6F4, one of the most-used fraction codes).** *Adding fractions* only added proper fractions, although the outcome list said it covered subtracting and mixed numbers. *Fixed:* from level 2 half of the questions take one fraction from another, and levels 4 and 5 start with a mixed number (answers are written as an improper fraction; no picture is drawn for mixed numbers).
5. **Overstated before:** the Year 6 outcome "multiply up to four digits by two digits" was marked fully covered by a topic that did not reach it, so was the Year 5 "four digits by one digit" division one. Both are true now.

Still partly covered (listed in the tables below): a stand-alone *estimate then check with the inverse* topic (4C3), correspondence and scaling problems (3C8), listing the combinations of two variables (6A5), and translating a shape in Year 4 (4P2).

Not yet compared and worth doing next: the *style* of the reasoning questions (how much reading, which contexts, how marks are given), the answer-first layout ("= 9,171 - 530") that the arithmetic paper uses, and the same exercise for GCSE and A-level papers.
