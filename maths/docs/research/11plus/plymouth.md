 School admissions 
Parents' guide

 1. Introduction 

 Normal round admissions 

 Most children are admitted at the normal point of entry for a school. This is generally the earliest point that children can enter the school: it varies according to the type of school:

 First, Infant and Primary schools – the Reception year
 Junior schools - Year 3
 Secondary schools - Year 7
 Key Stage 4 - Year 9
 Sixth Forms - Year 12

 When a school is newly opened or expands, it can also have a one-off normal point of entry. For example, a new primary school might open with children just in Reception, Year 1 and Year 2.

 This guide deals with normal round admissions and is aimed at parents of children who live in Plymouth.

 How the admissions system works 

 School admissions for the normal September intake are co-ordinated by local authorities across England to ensure that every child receives a single offer of a school place on the same national offer day. This system is designed to make school admissions fair, transparent and efficient by ensuring that every child who requires a school place is offered one, while preventing multiple offers being made to the same child.

 As part of the co-ordinated admissions process, parents and carers complete a single application form, even if they wish to apply for schools in different local authority areas. Parents are asked to list their preferred schools in order of preference. This is known as expressing a preference.

 Expressing a preference does not mean choosing a school outright. Instead, it indicates which schools’ parents would like their child to attend. Whether a place can be offered depends on the school's published admissions criteria, the number of places available and the number of applications received. The local authority will offer a place at the highest-ranked school on the application where the child meets the admissions criteria and a place is available.

 Plymouth City Council co-ordinates the admissions process for all state-funded schools in the city, but the criteria used to allocate places are determined by each school's admission authority. For community and voluntary controlled (VC) schools, Plymouth City Council is the admission authority. For academies, free schools, studio schools, University Technical Colleges (UTCs), foundation schools and voluntary aided (VA) schools, the admission authority is usually the governing body or academy trust. These schools are known as own admission authority schools.

 Own admission authority schools may have admissions policies that differ from Plymouth City Council's policy and may require a Supplementary Information Form (SIF). A SIF allows parents to provide additional information that may be relevant to a school's oversubscription criteria, such as evidence of faith, a parent's employment at the school, or medical or social reasons. If a required SIF is not submitted, the application will still be considered, but the child may not be assessed under all applicable criteria.

 Parents are strongly encouraged to read the admissions policy for every school they are considering. Understanding how applications are prioritised can help families make informed and realistic preferences. When a school receives more applications than there are places available, it is oversubscribed and places are allocated using the school's published admissions criteria.

 Parents should list schools in their genuine order of preference. If more than one preferred school can offer a place, the local authority will allocate the school ranked highest on the application. If none of the preferred schools can offer a place, an alternative school with available places will be offered, usually the nearest suitable school to the child's home.

 If a child is refused a place at a preferred school, parents have the right to appeal. Schools must also maintain a waiting list for at least the first term of the academic year. If a place becomes available, it will be offered according to the school's admissions criteria, not the date the application was received.

 For school admissions purposes, the term parent includes anyone with parental responsibility for a child, including carers and guardians.

 Plymouth City Council has a legal duty to ensure there are sufficient school places available for children living in the area within a reasonable distance of their home. Applications for the normal September intake must be submitted through the local authority, and all school place offers are communicated by the local authority rather than individual schools.

 All schools in Plymouth must comply with admissions legislation, the School Admissions Code, the School Admission Appeals Code, their published admissions policies and Plymouth City Council's co-ordinated admissions scheme.

 Choosing a school: choice and preference 

 As a parent in Plymouth, you have the option to name up to three schools on the common application form, listed in the order you’d most like your child to attend. Your top choice will be recorded as Preference one. While you're not required to name three schools, doing so increases the likelihood of your child receiving a school place, especially if your first preference is oversubscribed. Expressing a preference means indicating which school you’d like your child to attend—it isn’t a guarantee or a right to choose. Admission depends on a range of factors, including how many places are available and how your child meets the school’s criteria. That’s why it’s strongly recommended to include at least one local school near your home address, as a practical fallback.

 Equal ranked preferences and the application process 

 The application process enables parents to express a preference for any state-funded school in Plymouth or elsewhere in England. We will do our best to meet that preference by co-ordinating applications with schools.

 Each school has its own website, and most offer a prospectus, which you can get directly from the school. We recommend that parents review the information for any school they are considering as a preference.

 All school preferences on the application form are treated equally, regardless of whether they are ranked first, second, or third. If a child qualifies for a place at more than one preferred school, the offer will be made for the school the parent ranked highest that can provide a place. The coordinated admissions process ensures that, as far as possible, every parent who applies on time within a local authority area receives one offer of a school place on the same day.

 Admission authorities do not see the order in which parents rank their preferences because they do not need this information to make their decisions. This means parents can be confident that applying to a school farther from home will not harm their chances of getting a place at a closer school, such as their local school.

 We advise parents to list schools in the order of preference, starting with the school they most want their child to attend.

 Published admission number (PAN) 

 Every school must have a Published Admission Number (PAN) for its normal year of entry. The PAN is the number of places available for admission to that year group and represents the number of pupils the school considers it can accommodate.

 The PAN is the maximum number of places the school will offer for September admission, provided there is sufficient demand. Where a school has the capacity and resources to accommodate additional pupils, for example by creating an extra class, it may choose to increase its PAN. Any increase must be communicated to the local authority in sufficient time for the admissions process and published information to be updated.

 The local authority uses each school's PAN, together with the ranked lists of applicants provided in accordance with the school's admission arrangements, to allocate school places.

 When can a child start in a primary or infant school? 

 Children must be in full-time education from the start of the term following their fifth birthday, although this does not have to be at school as parents may choose to educate their child at home. School places for Reception are normally offered for full-time admission in September following a child's fourth birthday.

 When offered a Reception place, parents can:

 Accept a full-time place.
 Request part-time attendance until their child reaches compulsory school age.
 Defer admission until the start of the term following their fifth birthday within the same academic year.

 Parents wishing to defer admission must notify the headteacher of the allocated school. The place will be held until the child reaches compulsory school age within that academic year. If the school is not informed and the child does not start as expected, the place may be withdrawn.

 Children attending Reception part time cannot also access funded early years sessions at a pre-school setting, although additional childcare may be arranged at the parents' expense. While parents have the right to request part-time attendance, schools cannot be required to offer a particular attendance pattern.

 For summer-born children (born between 1 April and 31 August), parents may request that their child starts Reception a year later than normal. Requests are considered individually by the admission authority, taking account of the child's circumstances and any supporting evidence. If agreed, parents must reapply in the following admissions round, and the application will be considered alongside all other applicants. Approval to delay admission does not guarantee a place at a particular school.

 Parents may also request admission before September if their child has already reached their fourth birthday. Such requests are considered by the admission authority on an individual basis and there is no right of appeal against the decision.

 Class size limits 

 The law requires that no Key Stage 1 (infant) class (where the majority of children will have reached the age of five, six or seven by the end of the academic year) shall be a class of more than 30 pupils for any normal lesson with their teacher. Schools and the Council must ensure that children starting school in Plymouth do not join a class of more than 30 pupils. (Exceptions are allowed where, for example, an error has been made in implementing the admission arrangements, or the admission authority has acted “unreasonably” in refusing admission).

 Transfer To Junior, Secondary And Key Stage 4 Education 

 Children attending an infant school must transfer to another school at the end of Year 2, as infant schools do not admit pupils beyond this point. Junior schools provide the next stage of education from Year 3 onwards, and children attending a named feeder infant school are usually given priority for admission. However, any child in Year 2 may apply for a junior school place through the normal admissions process. Parents are not required to apply to their feeder junior school but must ensure suitable arrangements are in place for their child's education from Year 3. Applications for places at primary schools in Year 3 are treated as in-year admissions rather than part of the normal admissions round.

 Children must also transfer schools at the end of Year 6 to begin secondary education in Year 7. Plymouth has 18 secondary schools, including 15 non-selective schools and three grammar schools. Parents must apply for a secondary school place unless alternative educational arrangements have been made. Applications submitted after the closing date may be disadvantaged, as places are not reserved for late applicants. Parents whose child is being considered for a special school placement are advised to apply for a mainstream school place as a contingency.

 At the end of Key Stage 3, pupils choose the courses they will study at Key Stage 4. Most remain at their current secondary school, but parents may choose to apply for a place at Scott Medical and Healthcare College, Plymouth's studio school, which admits pupils at the start of Key Stage 4 and offers a combination of GCSE and specialist programmes. Applications are normally made between September and October of Year 8. Applying to transfer is entirely optional, and if no application is made, it will be assumed that the child will remain at their current secondary school. 

 Applying for a school place when your child has a special educational need or disability (SEND) 

 If your child receives SEND Support and does not have an Education, Health and Care Plan (EHCP), you should apply for a school place through the usual admissions process at www.plymouth.gov.uk/schooladmissions . If your child is undergoing an EHC needs assessment and a final EHCP has not yet been issued, you should still apply through the normal admissions process to ensure a school place is available while the assessment is being completed.

 If your child has an EHCP, you may still apply through the usual admissions process, but this is not essential as the EHCP process enables you to express a preference for a particular school or type of school. The local authority must name your preferred school unless it is unsuitable for your child's age, ability, aptitude or special educational needs, would negatively affect the education of other pupils, or would result in the inefficient use of resources.

 The 0-25 SEND Team will consult with your preferred school and any other appropriate schools before deciding which school to name in the EHCP. If you submit a school admissions application and indicate that your child has an EHCP, your application will be shared with the 0-25 SEND Team, who will manage the placement process.

 Before transferring to a new phase of education, your child's EHCP must be reviewed and, where necessary, amended through the annual review process. This review normally begins in the autumn term before the transfer year and gives parents the opportunity to comment on proposed changes to the EHCP and express a preference for the school they would like their child to attend. The local authority will then work with you to identify an appropriate school place.

 If you have any questions about this process, please contact the 0-25 SEND Team at: [email protected] 

 3. Making your application 

 How parents apply 

 Apply on our school admissions page .

 You can make changes to your application at any time before the closing date. On National Offer Day, you can view your school place offer online.

 The online application system is available 24 hours a day, allowing you to apply from home at a time that suits you. Once your application has been submitted, you will receive an email confirming that it has been received.

 To apply online, you will need to create a secure, password-protected account. If you log back into your account and make changes to your application, your original application will automatically be withdrawn. You must resubmit your application by selecting the Submit button. Once resubmitted, you will receive a new confirmation email confirming that your updated application has been received.

 Please note that we can only discuss your application with you, or with someone you have authorised in writing to act on your behalf.

 Late applications 

 If you miss the closing date, you can still submit an application using the form available on our School Admissions webpage .

 Applications received after the published deadline will be treated as late applications and will normally be processed only after all on-time applications have been considered. An exception may be made where there is a significant and valid reason for the delay, supported by appropriate evidence.

 Residency 

 Applications are assessed based on the address where the child lives. This address is especially important when a school is oversubscribed, as a child’s priority for a place is influenced by their home location.

 If a child lives at more than one address for roughly equal amounts of time, a decision will be made about which address to use for the application. Factors considered may include where child benefit is paid, where the child is registered with a GP, and other relevant information.

 The local authority may request proof of address and residency to verify the information provided. Please ensure your application is honest and up to date, as offers can be withdrawn if an application is found to be fraudulent or intentionally misleading.

 Moving house 

 You must inform us immediately if your address changes, even if you mentioned a future move on your application form. You will need to provide proof of your new address, such as a tenancy agreement (if applicable), council tax or utility bills, child tax credit or child benefit documents, or any other relevant information, including evidence that your previous home has been sold. It is very important to keep us updated, especially when you know you will be moving.

 Information on the application form 

 Each school's admission arrangements explain its oversubscription criteria and whether a Supplementary Information Form (SIF) is required. You should tell us if your child has a sibling at the school, has an Education, Health and Care Plan (EHCP), is currently a child in care or was previously a child in care, or if there is any other information relevant to your application.

 For some schools, a SIF must be completed if you wish to be considered for priority based on faith, staff connection, exceptional medical or social need, or Pupil Premium eligibility for grammar school applications.

 The admission authority can only consider information and supporting evidence provided with your application. This is particularly important for infant and primary school admissions, where appeals are subject to Infant Class Size legislation and new information cannot normally be considered at a later stage.

 Please ensure all information is accurate and complete. If false or misleading information is used to secure a place, the offer may be withdrawn.

 School places are allocated based on your child's home address. If your circumstances change, for example if you move house, you must inform us in writing as soon as possible. If we cannot contact you, your child's place may be offered to another applicant.

 If you no longer require an offered place, please let us know in writing as soon as possible.

 Other local authorities and further information 

 If you live in Plymouth and wish to apply for a school in a neighbouring local authority area, you must still submit your application through Plymouth City Council. Plymouth will share your preferences with the relevant local authority, which will assess the application and notify Plymouth of its decision.

 If you live outside Plymouth and wish to apply for a Plymouth school, you must apply through your home local authority. Your home authority will liaise with Plymouth City Council and will make any school place offer to you.

 Families moving to Plymouth from outside England may apply in advance of their move. However, applications cannot normally be considered until the child has entered the UK or evidence of a valid visa permitting entry has been provided.

 Some schools, particularly own admission authority schools, may also require a Supplementary Information Form (SIF). Parents should contact the school or relevant local authority for further information.

 5. How places are allocated 

 Priority for admission: oversubscription criteria 

 Below are the admission criteria definitions used by the different schools within the Plymouth area. Not all apply to each school - you can see which criteria each school uses on the Agreed School Admission Arrangements web page.

 Note: Meeting these criterions doesn’t guarantee a place, they influence how much priority a child has for a place at that school. Oversubscription criteria are reviewed annually and can change from one year to the next.

 Children in Care and formerly Children in Care 

 Children who have had a full or interim care order, or who are or have ever been accommodated under Section 20 or Section 22 of the Children Act 1989, are given the highest priority for admission to schools in Plymouth.

 This priority also applies to children who were immediately adopted or became the subject of a child arrangements order or special guardianship order when they ceased to be in care. Every state-funded school in Plymouth complies fully with this legal requirement.

 Applications for children in care or previously in care should be made by the person or local authority holding parental responsibility, rather than by a foster carer.

 Evidence that the child is, or has ever been, in care must be submitted with the application form. This should include a copy of the adoption order (if applicable) and/or a confirmation letter from the local authority that last looked after the child.

 Exceptional Need 

 Priority may be given where a parent can demonstrate an exceptional medical or social need for their child to attend a particular school, and that only that school can meet those needs. The need may relate to the child, the parent or carer, or both. 

 To be considered under this criterion, the circumstances must be exceptional and supported by independent professional evidence, such as from a medical practitioner, social worker, educational psychologist or other relevant professional. The evidence should explain the nature of the circumstances, why they are exceptional, and why the requested school is the only school able to meet those needs.

 Applications submitted without a completed Supplementary Information Form (SIF) and appropriate supporting evidence will not be considered under this criterion. It would not normally be expected for a parent or carer to claim exceptional medical or social need for a school that is not listed as their first preference.

 Priority under this criterion is granted in very few cases. Where an application is accepted as meeting the exceptional medical or social need criterion, it will be given the appropriate priority within the school's published admissions arrangements.

 Catchment area priority 

 Children living within a school’s designated catchment area may be prioritised higher in the admission criteria than those living outside it. However, only 5 out of the 70 primary schools in Plymouth use catchment areas as part of their admission criteria. The remaining schools—including all secondary schools—rely solely on the straight-line distance between the applicant’s home address and the school as a tie-breaker between admission categories.

 The primary schools that still operate a catchment area system are:

 St Andrew’s CE VA Primary School
 St Budeaux Foundation CE Primary School
 St George’s CE Primary Academy
 St Matthew’s CE Primary and Nursery Academy
 St Peter’s CE Primary School

 Children of staff priority 

 Children whose parent or carer is a member of staff employed on a permanent contract at the school for two or more years at the time the application for admission is made, or where the staff member is recruited to fill a vacant post with a demonstrable skill shortage (as evidenced by completion of the staff supplementary information form) may receive priority in admissions. This applies to all staff working directly at the school to which the application relates but does not include staff who work on the school site for other employers.

 Sibling priority 

 The definition of “sibling” may vary from school to school. Children may be considered siblings if they live in the same household in a single-family unit. This may include full, half, step, or adoptive brothers or sisters. In the case where parents have children attending linked infant or junior schools, a sibling connection may be considered. For the exact definition of a sibling used, please refer to the individual school’s admission criteria.

 If there is a shared care arrangement, please provide a copy of a solicitor’s letter or Court Order confirming that the children live together, including details of their main carer and home address, as proof that they are siblings.

 Faith priority 

 Voluntary aided, voluntary controlled schools, and some academies have a recognised faith character and may prioritise applicants based on faith grounds. This is typically defined as regular attendance—at least once a month for the two years immediately prior to the application—supported by evidence from parents or carers and the church minister. This information is verified through a school Supplementary Information Form (SIF).

 Feeder school priority 

 There is no automatic provision within the School Admissions Code to give priority to children who attend a nursery school. The School Admissions Code states:

 1.42 Admission authorities may give priority in their oversubscription criteria to children eligible for the early year’s pupil premium, the pupil premium, or the service premium who:

 a) are in a nursery class which is part of the school; or

b) attend a nursery that is established and run by the school. The nursery must be named in the admission arrangements, and its selection must be transparent and made on reasonable grounds.

 Most secondary and junior schools give priority to children who would join them from named linked or feeder schools. Junior schools name one infant school; secondary schools can name several schools. This applies only at the normal round.

 For feeder linked schools visit Secondary school feeder links .

 Pupil premium priority 

 Schools may give priority to children who qualify for the Pupil Premium, Service Pupil Premium, or Early Years Pupil Premium as part of their admission criteria.

 All three Plymouth grammar schools include this in their admissions process. Eligibility is confirmed through the completion of a Supplementary Information Form (SIF).

 Other 

 Children who do not fall into any of the other admission categories will be considered under the “other” category. In these cases, places are allocated based solely on the straight-line distance from the child’s home to the school.

 Tie breaker 

 If a school is oversubscribed—that is, it receives more applications than the number of available places—priority within each admissions category will be given to children who live closer to the school. Distance is measured in a straight-line using Plymouth City Council’s electronic mapping system.

 Measurements are taken from the spatial locator for both the child’s home and the school, as defined by the National Land and Property Gazetteer. This point is usually a central reference point within the property. In the case of flats, all residences within the building are measured from the same central point, regardless of which floor they are on.

 If two or more children live the same distance from the school and there are not enough places to offer to all of them, a random ballot will be used to decide who is offered a place. If the school has delegated this process to the local authority, the ballot will be supervised by an Admissions Officer and the School Admissions Manager.

 Selective Schools 

 All primary schools in Plymouth are comprehensive, meaning they do not use selection tests. This is also true for most secondary schools, except for Devonport High School for Boys, Devonport High School for Girls, and Plymouth High School for Girls, which admit students based on academic ability demonstrated through an entrance exam.

 These grammar schools require children to take the 11+ entrance exam in Year 6 to assess grammar school eligibility. Passing the exam does not guarantee admission, as these schools are often oversubscribed. However, all three schools maintain waiting lists.

 Other points to consider 

 Sibling 

 If you have another child who is offered a place at the same school after the application deadline but before the processing date, and you inform us in writing, that child will be counted as a sibling for this application.

 Multiple birth siblings 

 There is a general presumption in favour of admitting all siblings if one can be offered a place, but this is not guaranteed. If only one child can be offered the final place, we will try to offer places to the other sibling(s) as well. However, a school may not be able to exceed its Published Admission Number (PAN). If this happens, it will be up to you to decide whether to accept the place for one child or to seek places for all your children at a different school.

 Children of UK service personnel 

 Applications for children of UK service personnel who are moving to the area must include an official government letter confirming the relocation date and a Unit postal or quartering area address. This information will be used to assess the application under the school’s oversubscription criteria. However, there is no additional priority given to service families, and in line with the School Admissions Code, places will not be reserved in advance for families who may move into the area.

 Parents with shared responsibility 

 Only one home address will be accepted for each child. If necessary, each admission authority will decide what it considers to be the child’s home address, in line with its own admissions policy. In some cases, this may be determined by a court order. The admission authority will take into account all information provided by either parent.

 In rare cases, parents may submit applications for different schools. This can be distressing for the child, and we strongly encourage parents to come to an agreement that serves the child's best interests. If no agreement is reached and there is no court order in place, a school place will not be held open at one school once the child has started at another.

 Disputes between parents should be resolved through mediation or the courts . Neither the local authority nor the school will get involved in these matters.

 6. What happens after you apply 

 We will check the information on your application against the data we already hold. For Plymouth community and voluntary controlled schools, as well as academies that use our admissions services, we will rank the preferences directly. For other Plymouth schools that manage their own admissions, we will send them the details of your application for consideration.

 If you’ve applied for schools outside Plymouth, we will forward your application details to the relevant local authority, who will then pass them on to the appropriate admission authorities.

 Schools do not know whether you listed them as your first, second, or third preference, as this information is not needed to apply their oversubscription criteria.

Each child will be offered a place at the highest-ranked school that can offer them a place. If none of your preferences can be met, we will offer a place at an alternative school.

 1. Introduction 

 Normal round admissions 

 Most children are admitted at the normal point of entry for a school. This is generally the earliest point that children can enter the school: it varies according to the type of school:

 First, Infant and Primary schools – the Reception year
 Junior schools - Year 3
 Secondary schools - Year 7
 Key Stage 4 - Year 9
 Sixth Forms - Year 12

 When a school is newly opened or expands, it can also have a one-off normal point of entry. For example, a new primary school might open with children just in Reception, Year 1 and Year 2.

 This guide deals with normal round admissions and is aimed at parents of children who live in Plymouth.

 How the admissions system works 

 School admissions for the normal September intake are co-ordinated by local authorities across England to ensure that every child receives a single offer of a school place on the same national offer day. This system is designed to make school admissions fair, transparent and efficient by ensuring that every child who requires a school place is offered one, while preventing multiple offers being made to the same child.

 As part of the co-ordinated admissions process, parents and carers complete a single application form, even if they wish to apply for schools in different local authority areas. Parents are asked to list their preferred schools in order of preference. This is known as expressing a preference.

 Expressing a preference does not mean choosing a school outright. Instead, it indicates which schools’ parents would like their child to attend. Whether a place can be offered depends on the school's published admissions criteria, the number of places available and the number of applications received. The local authority will offer a place at the highest-ranked school on the application where the child meets the admissions criteria and a place is available.

 Plymouth City Council co-ordinates the admissions process for all state-funded schools in the city, but the criteria used to allocate places are determined by each school's admission authority. For community and voluntary controlled (VC) schools, Plymouth City Council is the admission authority. For academies, free schools, studio schools, University Technical Colleges (UTCs), foundation schools and voluntary aided (VA) schools, the admission authority is usually the governing body or academy trust. These schools are known as own admission authority schools.

 Own admission authority schools may have admissions policies that differ from Plymouth City Council's policy and may require a Supplementary Information Form (SIF). A SIF allows parents to provide additional information that may be relevant to a school's oversubscription criteria, such as evidence of faith, a parent's employment at the school, or medical or social reasons. If a required SIF is not submitted, the application will still be considered, but the child may not be assessed under all applicable criteria.

 Parents are strongly encouraged to read the admissions policy for every school they are considering. Understanding how applications are prioritised can help families make informed and realistic preferences. When a school receives more applications than there are places available, it is oversubscribed and places are allocated using the school's published admissions criteria.

 Parents should list schools in their genuine order of preference. If more than one preferred school can offer a place, the local authority will allocate the school ranked highest on the application. If none of the preferred schools can offer a place, an alternative school with available places will be offered, usually the nearest suitable school to the child's home.

 If a child is refused a place at a preferred school, parents have the right to appeal. Schools must also maintain a waiting list for at least the first term of the academic year. If a place becomes available, it will be offered according to the school's admissions criteria, not the date the application was received.

 For school admissions purposes, the term parent includes anyone with parental responsibility for a child, including carers and guardians.

 Plymouth City Council has a legal duty to ensure there are sufficient school places available for children living in the area within a reasonable distance of their home. Applications for the normal September intake must be submitted through the local authority, and all school place offers are communicated by the local authority rather than individual schools.

 All schools in Plymouth must comply with admissions legislation, the School Admissions Code, the School Admission Appeals Code, their published admissions policies and Plymouth City Council's co-ordinated admissions scheme.

 Choosing a school: choice and preference 

 As a parent in Plymouth, you have the option to name up to three schools on the common application form, listed in the order you’d most like your child to attend. Your top choice will be recorded as Preference one. While you're not required to name three schools, doing so increases the likelihood of your child receiving a school place, especially if your first preference is oversubscribed. Expressing a preference means indicating which school you’d like your child to attend—it isn’t a guarantee or a right to choose. Admission depends on a range of factors, including how many places are available and how your child meets the school’s criteria. That’s why it’s strongly recommended to include at least one local school near your home address, as a practical fallback.

 Equal ranked preferences and the application process 

 The application process enables parents to express a preference for any state-funded school in Plymouth or elsewhere in England. We will do our best to meet that preference by co-ordinating applications with schools.

 Each school has its own website, and most offer a prospectus, which you can get directly from the school. We recommend that parents review the information for any school they are considering as a preference.

 All school preferences on the application form are treated equally, regardless of whether they are ranked first, second, or third. If a child qualifies for a place at more than one preferred school, the offer will be made for the school the parent ranked highest that can provide a place. The coordinated admissions process ensures that, as far as possible, every parent who applies on time within a local authority area receives one offer of a school place on the same day.

 Admission authorities do not see the order in which parents rank their preferences because they do not need this information to make their decisions. This means parents can be confident that applying to a school farther from home will not harm their chances of getting a place at a closer school, such as their local school.

 We advise parents to list schools in the order of preference, starting with the school they most want their child to attend.

 Published admission number (PAN) 

 Every school must have a Published Admission Number (PAN) for its normal year of entry. The PAN is the number of places available for admission to that year group and represents the number of pupils the school considers it can accommodate.

 The PAN is the maximum number of places the school will offer for September admission, provided there is sufficient demand. Where a school has the capacity and resources to accommodate additional pupils, for example by creating an extra class, it may choose to increase its PAN. Any increase must be communicated to the local authority in sufficient time for the admissions process and published information to be updated.

 The local authority uses each school's PAN, together with the ranked lists of applicants provided in accordance with the school's admission arrangements, to allocate school places.

 When can a child start in a primary or infant school?

 Accept a full-time place.
 Request part-time attendance until their child reaches compulsory school age.

Approval to delay admission does not guarantee a place at a particular school.

 Class size limits 

 The law requires that no Key Stage 1 (infant) class (where the majority of children will have reached the age of five, six or seven by the end of the academic year) shall be a class of more than 30 pupils for any normal lesson with their teacher. Parents whose child is being considered for a special school placement are advised to apply for a mainstream school place as a contingency.

 5. How places are allocated 

 Priority for admission: oversubscription criteria 

 Below are the admission criteria definitions used by the different schools within the Plymouth area. Not all apply to each school - you can see which criteria each school uses on the Agreed School Admission Arrangements web page.

 Note: Meeting these criterions doesn’t guarantee a place, they influence how much priority a child has for a place at that school. Oversubscription criteria are reviewed annually and can change from one year to the next.

 Children in Care and formerly Children in Care 

 Children who have had a full or interim care order, or who are or have ever been accommodated under Section 20 or Section 22 of the Children Act 1989, are given the highest priority for admission to schools in Plymouth.

 This priority also applies to children who were immediately adopted or became the subject of a child arrangements order or special guardianship order when they ceased to be in care. Every state-funded school in Plymouth complies fully with this legal requirement.

 Applications for children in care or previously in care should be made by the person or local authority holding parental responsibility, rather than by a foster carer.

 Evidence that the child is, or has ever been, in care must be submitted with the application form. This should include a copy of the adoption order (if applicable) and/or a confirmation letter from the local authority that last looked after the child.

 Exceptional Need 

 Priority may be given where a parent can demonstrate an exceptional medical or social need for their child to attend a particular school, and that only that school can meet those needs. The need may relate to the child, the parent or carer, or both. 

 To be considered under this criterion, the circumstances must be exceptional and supported by independent professional evidence, such as from a medical practitioner, social worker, educational psychologist or other relevant professional. The evidence should explain the nature of the circumstances, why they are exceptional, and why the requested school is the only school able to meet those needs.

 Applications submitted without a completed Supplementary Information Form (SIF) and appropriate supporting evidence will not be considered under this criterion. It would not normally be expected for a parent or carer to claim exceptional medical or social need for a school that is not listed as their first preference.

 Priority under this criterion is granted in very few cases. Where an application is accepted as meeting the exceptional medical or social need criterion, it will be given the appropriate priority within the school's published admissions arrangements.

 Catchment area priority 

 Children living within a school’s designated catchment area may be prioritised higher in the admission criteria than those living outside it. However, only 5 out of the 70 primary schools in Plymouth use catchment areas as part of their admission criteria. The remaining schools—including all secondary schools—rely solely on the straight-line distance between the applicant’s home address and the school as a tie-breaker between admission categories.

 The primary schools that still operate a catchment area system are:

 St Andrew’s CE VA Primary School
 St Budeaux Foundation CE Primary School
 St George’s CE Primary Academy
 St Matthew’s CE Primary and Nursery Academy
 St Peter’s CE Primary School

 Children of staff priority 

 Children whose parent or carer is a member of staff employed on a permanent contract at the school for two or more years at the time the application for admission is made, or where the staff member is recruited to fill a vacant post with a demonstrable skill shortage (as evidenced by completion of the staff supplementary information form) may receive priority in admissions. This applies to all staff working directly at the school to which the application relates but does not include staff who work on the school site for other employers.

 Sibling priority 

 The definition of “sibling” may vary from school to school. Children may be considered siblings if they live in the same household in a single-family unit. This may include full, half, step, or adoptive brothers or sisters. In the case where parents have children attending linked infant or junior schools, a sibling connection may be considered. For the exact definition of a sibling used, please refer to the individual school’s admission criteria.

 If there is a shared care arrangement, please provide a copy of a solicitor’s letter or Court Order confirming that the children live together, including details of their main carer and home address, as proof that they are siblings.

 Faith priority 

 Voluntary aided, voluntary controlled schools, and some academies have a recognised faith character and may prioritise applicants based on faith grounds. This is typically defined as regular attendance—at least once a month for the two years immediately prior to the application—supported by evidence from parents or carers and the church minister. This information is verified through a school Supplementary Information Form (SIF).

 Feeder school priority 

 There is no automatic provision within the School Admissions Code to give priority to children who attend a nursery school. The School Admissions Code states:

 1.42 Admission authorities may give priority in their oversubscription criteria to children eligible for the early year’s pupil premium, the pupil premium, or the service premium who:

 a) are in a nursery class which is part of the school; or

b) attend a nursery that is established and run by the school. The nursery must be named in the admission arrangements, and its selection must be transparent and made on reasonable grounds.

 Most secondary and junior schools give priority to children who would join them from named linked or feeder schools. Junior schools name one infant school; secondary schools can name several schools. This applies only at the normal round.

 For feeder linked schools visit Secondary school feeder links .

 Pupil premium priority 

 Schools may give priority to children who qualify for the Pupil Premium, Service Pupil Premium, or Early Years Pupil Premium as part of their admission criteria.

 All three Plymouth grammar schools include this in their admissions process. Eligibility is confirmed through the completion of a Supplementary Information Form (SIF).

 Other 

 Children who do not fall into any of the other admission categories will be considered under the “other” category. In these cases, places are allocated based solely on the straight-line distance from the child’s home to the school.

 Tie breaker 

 If a school is oversubscribed—that is, it receives more applications than the number of available places—priority within each admissions category will be given to children who live closer to the school. Distance is measured in a straight-line using Plymouth City Council’s electronic mapping system.

 Measurements are taken from the spatial locator for both the child’s home and the school, as defined by the National Land and Property Gazetteer. This point is usually a central reference point within the property. In the case of flats, all residences within the building are measured from the same central point, regardless of which floor they are on.

 If two or more children live the same distance from the school and there are not enough places to offer to all of them, a random ballot will be used to decide who is offered a place. If the school has delegated this process to the local authority, the ballot will be supervised by an Admissions Officer and the School Admissions Manager.

 Selective Schools 

 All primary schools in Plymouth are comprehensive, meaning they do not use selection tests. This is also true for most secondary schools, except for Devonport High School for Boys, Devonport High School for Girls, and Plymouth High School for Girls, which admit students based on academic ability demonstrated through an entrance exam.

 These grammar schools require children to take the 11+ entrance exam in Year 6 to assess grammar school eligibility. Passing the exam does not guarantee admission, as these schools are often oversubscribed. However, all three schools maintain waiting lists.

 The 11+ examination will take place over two Saturdays within September:

 Saturday 12 September 2026
 Saturday 19 September 2026

 Other points to consider 

 Sibling 

 If you have another child who is offered a place at the same school after the application deadline but before the processing date, and you inform us in writing, that child will be counted as a sibling for this application.

 Multiple birth siblings 

 There is a general presumption in favour of admitting all siblings if one can be offered a place, but this is not guaranteed. If only one child can be offered the final place, we will try to offer places to the other sibling(s) as well. However, a school may not be able to exceed its Published Admission Number (PAN). If this happens, it will be up to you to decide whether to accept the place for one child or to seek places for all your children at a different school.

 Children of UK service personnel 

 Applications for children of UK service personnel who are moving to the area must include an official government letter confirming the relocation date and a Unit postal or quartering area address. This information will be used to assess the application under the school’s oversubscription criteria. However, there is no additional priority given to service families, and in line with the School Admissions Code, places will not be reserved in advance for families who may move into the area.

 Parents with shared responsibility 

 Only one home address will be accepted for each child. If necessary, each admission authority will decide what it considers to be the child’s home address, in line with its own admissions policy. In some cases, this may be determined by a court order. The admission authority will take into account all information provided by either parent.

 In rare cases, parents may submit applications for different schools. This can be distressing for the child, and we strongly encourage parents to come to an agreement that serves the child's best interests. If no agreement is reached and there is no court order in place, a school place will not be held open at one school once the child has started at another.

 Disputes between parents should be resolved through mediation or the courts . Neither the local authority nor the school will get involved in these matters.

 6. What happens after you apply 

 We will check the information on your application against the data we already hold. For Plymouth community and voluntary controlled schools, as well as academies that use our admissions services, we will rank the preferences directly. For other Plymouth schools that manage their own admissions, we will send them the details of your application for consideration.

 If you’ve applied for schools outside Plymouth, we will forward your application details to the relevant local authority, who will then pass them on to the appropriate admission authorities.

 Schools do not know whether you listed them as your first, second, or third preference, as this information is not needed to apply their oversubscription criteria.

 Once we receive the ranked lists back from schools and other local authorities, we will use them to process all applications. Each child will be offered a place at the highest-ranked school that can offer them a place. If none of your preferences can be met, we will offer a place at an alternative school.

 When will you know the result? 

 On the National Offer Date for primary and secondary places.

 Offers of secondary school places are made on the National Allocation Date (1 st March or the next working day if this falls on a weekend or public holiday) each year.
 Offers of primary school places are made on the National Allocation Date (16 th April or the next working day if this falls on a weekend or public holiday) each year.

 You will receive an email on the offer date. It is your responsibility to keep your email address updated. You can also log into your online account on the offer date to find out which school has been offered.

 What to do when you receive an offer? 

 You must let us know within seven days whether you accept or decline the school place offered.

 You can respond by logging into your online account.
 If you prefer, or if you didn’t apply online or live in another local authority, you can reply by email.
 If you accept the offered place, your child will remain on waiting lists for any higher-ranked schools. If a place becomes available at one of those schools, your child will be offered that place instead, overriding your previous acceptance.
 If you decline the offer, you must tell us in writing.
 If you no longer want to stay on a waiting list, you must also inform us in writing.
 When you reject a place, it will be offered to another child.
 Responding directly to the school does not count as accepting the offer—you must reply to the local authority.
 If we do not receive your response within seven days, the place may be withdrawn and may be offered to someone else.
 Your child will automatically remain on the waiting lists for any schools ranked higher than the school offered. If a place becomes available at a higher preference school, a new offer will be made and will automatically replace your original offer, even if you have already accepted it. The previously allocated place will then be offered to another child. If you no longer wish your child to remain on a waiting list for a higher preference school, you must notify us in writing.

 7. If you are unsuccessful 

 Waiting list 

 If your child is refused a place at any school, they will automatically be placed on the waiting list for that school. Waiting lists are maintained in accordance with each school’s published admission criteria. The School Admissions Code states in respect of waiting lists:

 Each admission authority must maintain a clear, fair and objective waiting list until at least 31 December of each school year (most schools hold waiting lists for longer periods than that stated in the Code)
 Priority must not be given to children based on the date their application was received, or their name was added to the list.
 Looked after children, previously looked after children, and those allocated a place at the school in accordance with a Fair Access Protocol, must take precedence over those on a waiting list.

 While parents may not agree with how the waiting list is arranged, it is structured in accordance with the law to ensure a fair and consistent admissions process.

 If a place becomes available, your application will be considered alongside others on the waiting list, any appeal cases, and any late applications, in accordance with the published admission criteria.

 If your child is eligible for a place, we will allocate that place to your child as you ranked the school higher than the one allocated. We will reallocate the place previously held to someone else regardless of whether or not you have notified us of your acceptance of the allocation.

 If you do not wish to remain on a waiting list, you must put this in writing to us. The waiting list will be maintained until 31 August 2027. From 1 September 2027 the In-year admissions criteria will apply.

 In regard to secondary school lists, they are recalculated for the autumn term to remove priority for children who attended a linked or feeder school as this is not relevant to In-Year admissions.

 11 Plus Practice Examination

 Plymouth High School will be running a Practice 11 Plus test on Saturday 3rd July 2027. 

 **Registration currently closed** Link to 11 Plus Practice Exam Registration Form 

 Registration will open from 12 o'clock noon on Monday 12th April 2027 and will close when all 170 places are filled or at 3pm on the 26th May 2027, whichever is soonest. 

 The cost of sitting the examination is £50.00. The online application form contains details about how to pay for the examination. Please note, it may take a few days for your payment to be acknowledged due to staff working days. The cost of the examination is non-refundable.

 The results of the 11 Plus Practice Examination results will be emailed by the end of the Summer term 2027. 

 Please note: 

 You do not have to take the 11 Plus Practice Examination in July in order to take the real 11 Plus Examination in September. 

 Registration for the 11 Plus Practice Examination does not automatically register your child for the real 11 Plus Examination. There is a separate registration process for each examination.
