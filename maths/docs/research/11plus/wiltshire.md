 What Does the 11+ Test Involve?

 SWGS uses GL Assessment as their 11+ test provider. The test consists of three papers taken in one sitting with a break in-between each paper:

 Mathematics (50minutes) covering all aspects of the year 5 National Curriculum in mathematics with some questions covering the start of the year 6 curriculum.

 English (45minutes) assessing linguistic skills as well as comprehension, spelling and punctuation

 Verbal Reasoning (50minutes) assessing logical thinking and problem-solving skills

 Free familiarisation papers from GL Assessment are available here and will help students to become familiar with the multiple-choice layout of the test, content and examples of some types of questions they will face. This is particularly useful for the verbal reasoning tests as students may not have encountered this type of question before. The number of questions in each test may vary.

 Exam Results

 A Level Results 2026

 SWGS is celebrating another year of exceptional A Level results, with 218 students completing their examinations this summer. An impressive 31% of all grades were awarded at A or A*, while 31 students achieved a clean sweep of A*/A grades across at least three subjects.

 These outstanding results have enabled the vast majority of students to secure places at their first-choice universities, pursuing a diverse range of academic and professional pathways. Students will be progressing to highly prestigious institutions and courses, including Medicine, Mathematics, Professional Dance and Musical Theatre at the renowned Bird College Conservatoire. In addition, one student has secured a place at the Royal Welsh Conservatoire, while several others have gained places on highly competitive apprenticeship programmes in finance, law, accountancy, and the Royal Navy.

 Commenting on this year’s achievements, Headteacher Dr Amanda Smith said:

 “We are immensely proud of every student’s hard work and determination. Achieving these results with our largest-ever cohort is a remarkable accomplishment. It is a testament to the dedication of our staff and students, whose collective efforts have created a supportive, inclusive and academically ambitious environment where all learners can succeed.” 

 As we bid farewell to another successful cohort, we wish our students every success in their future studies, careers, and lives beyond school. At the same time, we look forward to welcoming the next generation of Sixth Form students and supporting them as they strive to achieve their own ambitions and fulfil their potential.

 GCSE Results 2026

 South Wilts Grammar School is proud to celebrate an exceptional set of GCSE results, reflecting the talent, dedication and hard work of its students. Across a broad range of subjects, this year’s cohort has achieved remarkable success, continuing the school’s long-standing tradition of academic excellence.

 This year, 155 students completed their GCSE examinations, with an impressive 74% of all grades awarded at 7 to 9, including 24% at the highest grade 9. The school’s provisional Progress 8 score of +1.32 further highlights the exceptional progress students have made throughout their time at South Wilts Grammar School. These outstanding outcomes are a testament to the commitment, determination and ambition demonstrated by students throughout their studies.

 In addition to the exceptional progress and attainment achieved across the year group, six students achieved grade 9s in at least ten subjects, while 57 students secured eight or more grades at 8 or 9. These results demonstrate both individual excellence and the strength of performance across the cohort.

 Headteacher Dr Amanda Smith said:

 “We are absolutely delighted with these outstanding results and immensely proud of every one of our students. Their achievements reflect years of hard work, perseverance and ambition, and they deserve enormous credit for all they have accomplished. These results are not only a celebration of academic success, but also of the resilience, kindness and support that this year group has shown throughout their time at South Wilts Grammar School. 

 I would like to thank our dedicated staff and supportive families, whose encouragement and commitment have played such an important role in helping our students reach their potential. It has been a privilege to watch these young people grow into confident, capable and compassionate individuals, and we look forward to welcoming many of them into our Sixth Form as they continue their journey with us.” 

 The whole school community is celebrating these fantastic achievements and congratulates all students on their success. We wish them every happiness and success as they take their next steps and look forward to seeing all they will accomplish in the future.

 Frequently Asked Questions

 What is the purpose of the 11+ selection procedure?

 The Eleven plus selection procedure is designed to determine whether or not children in the autumn term of their final year at primary school are suitable for admission to a grammar school. Parents should note that meeting the academic requirements for entry to the School is not, in itself, a guarantee of an offer of a place.

 How do I register my son for the 11+ Test?

 In order to register, the School's 11+ Application Form and the Local Authority Common or Secondary Application form (CAF or SAF) must both be completed.

 It is the responsibility of parents/guardians to ensure that they register with their Local Authorities by the given deadline.

 My son is (or has previously been) entitled to Free School Meals. How does this affect an application?

 BWS is committed to widening learners from all backgrounds. Boys with parents/guardians on low incomes are entitled to priority when places are allocated and have a lowered qualifying standard.

 Applicants who, at the time of the entry exam, meet the eligibility criteria to claim for free school meals currently or in any of the previous six years and who therefore qualify for the Pupil Premium Grant must indicate that their son qualifies for Free School Meals on the 11+ Registration Form and provide documentary evidence.

 From September 2026 the qualifying standard for boys who qualify as Pupil Premium will be 10% lower. In addition 10 places are reserved for them. To qualify for a modified qualifying standard supporting documentary evidence from the local authority will be required. If fewer than 10 pupil premium eligible boys meet the reduced qualifying standard, then the places will be reallocated to standard qualifying standard applicants.

 For information on free or subsidised resources to prepare for the 11+ exam please see here 

 Does the Eleven Plus Procedure take account of a Special Educational Needs, an Educational Health Plan or other barriers to learning?

 Yes. If a pupil has a current statement of educational needs (SEN) including an Education Health Care Plan (EHCP), speaks English as a Second Language, or faces any other barrier to learning, whether medical or educational, or in that event that parents believe his circumstances may affect his performance in the entrance exam, they can request that reasonable adjustments are put in place. 

 There is more information on the Special Access Arrangements page .

 Does it matter where we live?

 For practical reasons we recommend that a pupil’s journey time should be under an hour in order that he can fully participate in the wider life of the school.

 The current designated area map for Bishop Wordsworth's School is shown below (higher resolution copy at the bottom of the page).

 Boys who live within the designated area will be given priority in the event of oversubscription. Boys do not need to live within the designated area to apply or attend BWS.

 From September 2026: In the event of excess applications to sit the 11+, the 480 boys who live closest to the school's Exeter Street gate will be prioritised. Please see the Admissions Policy 2027-2028 for more information.

 Is there financial assistance available?

 The BWS Foundation provides grants to pupils not currently at the School but who need financial assistance to support their application, for example attending open days and the 11+ examination. For information on how to apply, please see our Financial Assistance page.

 For information on free or subsidised resources to prepare for the 11+ exam please see here .

 My son qualifies for the Service Premium. Does this affect an application?

 Boys whose parent is or has been a member of the Regular Armed Forces are entitled to priority when places are allocated. Parents must indicate on the 11+ Registration form that their son qualifies for the Service Premium in their school or that their son has been a Service Child in any of the previous three years prior to the date of the test or that their son is in receipt of a child pension from the Ministry of Defence. Documentary evidence will be required.

 How are the 11+ tests marked?

 Our assessment provider writes the mark scheme and scripts are read by OMR to ensure accuracy. All marks are 'standardised' according to the age in years and months of the applicant to produce a final score.

 If my son 'passes' the 11+ will he be offered a place?

 Your results letter from the School will inform you if your son has met the qualifying standard but this letter does not constitute an offer of a place. Offers depend not only on meeting the qualifying standard but also on the overall number of boys who passed and the preferences that parents have expressed on the Secondary (sometimes called ’Common’) Application Form issued by the Local Authority. If the school is, as usual, oversubscribed, boys who have met the qualifying standard will not be offered places but will be put on the waiting list. More detail can be found in the Admissions Policy .

 If my son is not offered a place, is it possible to appeal?

 Please see the Appeals page for more information.
