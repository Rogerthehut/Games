 Applying to Bournemouth School
 Securing a place at Bournemouth School requires understanding both the local authority's application process and the specific entry requirements of the school. As a selective grammar school, admission into Year 7 is based on academic ability, determined by an entrance examination commonly referred to as the 11-plus.
 This page provides an independent overview of how the admissions cycle works. Please note that for the authoritative admissions policy, precise catchment area details, and the official Published Admission Number (PAN) for the upcoming year, you must refer to the school's official website .
 The Selective Entrance Test (11-Plus)
 Because Bournemouth School is a grammar school, boys wishing to attend must demonstrate they have the required academic aptitude. This is assessed via the entrance test, which boys sit early in Year 6 (typically in September). Parents must register their child to take this test directly with the school well before the end of Year 5.
 The tests usually cover English, Mathematics, and Verbal Reasoning, designed to identify pupils who will thrive in the fast-paced, rigorous academic environment that leads to the school's high Attainment 8 and Progress 8 scores. Only boys who meet the required standard in these tests are eligible to be considered for a place.
 The BCP Coordinated Admissions Scheme
 Sitting and passing the entrance test does not guarantee a place; you must still formally apply through your local authority. Bournemouth School participates in the BCP (Bournemouth, Christchurch, and Poole) coordinated admissions scheme. This means that if you live in the BCP area, you submit one application form to BCP Council listing your preferred schools in order. If you live outside BCP (e.g., in Dorset or Hampshire), you apply through your own "home" local authority, and they will coordinate with BCP.
 Key Application Timeline
 Spring/Summer of Year 5: Register your son to sit the entrance test.
 September of Year 6: Boys sit the entrance examination.
 October of Year 6: Parents receive the test results indicating if their son is eligible.
 31 October: National deadline to submit the secondary school application to your local authority.
 1 March: National Offer Day. Local authorities inform parents which school their child has been allocated.

 Oversubscription and Distance
 Every year, more boys pass the entrance test than there are places available (the school's overall capacity is 1100). When this happens, Bournemouth School applies its oversubscription criteria to determine who gets a place.
 Historically, priority is given to Looked After Children and boys eligible for the Pupil Premium (those registered for Free School Meals, who currently make up 6.7% of the school). After these criteria, places are typically allocated based on the rank order of the entrance test scores, and then by straight-line distance from the boy's home address to the school.
 Because oversubscription criteria can change, you should always check the exact wording of the policy for your son's year of entry via the school or the BCP Council admissions portal. If you are not offered a place on 1 March, you have the statutory right to appeal the decision during the spring and summer terms.

 Preparing for the entrance tests for September 2026 

 ENTRANCE TESTS  
 Pupils seeking admission into Year 7 take tests in Mathematics, English and Verbal Reasoning.
  All tests are set to be appropriate for the pupils in their final year of the KS2 national 
curriculum. It is acknowledged that candidates are unlikely to have covered all of the work that is tested – the tests take account of this 
 Mathematics (GL Assessment) – Time Allowed: 50 minutes 
 The Mathematics paper is based on work which candidates would normally cover during Key Stage 2 of the national curriculum. It covers most aspects of Key Stage 2, up to level 5, with some questions aimed at level 6.
 English (GL Assessment) –  Time Allowed: 45 minutes
 The English paper tests comprehension, vocabulary and punctuation.
 Verbal Reasoning (GL Assessment) -Time Allowed: 50 minutes 
This test is designed to analyse a pupil’s academic potential and indicate areas of strength or weakness.
 GL Assessment Papers  
For the three GL Assessment papers, questions are of a multiple-choice style, with candidates entering their answers on a pre-printed sheet. Scores achieved in these papers are standardised against national criteria by age.
 All GL Assessment tests are designed to be taken to very strict time limits. Many pupils within a wide range of ability will be able to answer most of the questions, given sufficient time; our tests show a pupil’s ability to work accurately at speed, a vital aspect of their future work in a selective school. For this reason, no variation in this timing is permitted unless the applicant has a relevant statement of educational need. Any specific problems which may affect a girl’s performance should be drawn to the attention of the Access Arrangements Panel by submitting the Application for Access Arrangements form. The deadline for submitting this form is 12 noon on Monday 7th September 2026. The Access Arrangements Panel will take all such information into account when deciding on eligibility for entry to the school.
